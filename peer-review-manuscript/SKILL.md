# Peer review a manuscript

Help the author decide what the draft establishes and what most needs to change before submission. Follow the requested journal, scope, and report format when given. For an independent reanalysis of an empirical article's results, use `review-article`.

Read the manuscript and available supplements, including the exhibits and central derivations. Identify its question, main claim, evidence, and intended audience. State any access limit that narrows the review.

Judge **correctness**, **originality**, and **value** separately. Check decisive claims against the paper and compare novelty claims with the closest primary literature. Distinguish a demonstrated error from a concern or an unverified possibility; distinguish an illustration from evidence on observed truth and a partial result from a usable method.

Lead with the few issues that could change the assessment. For each, give a manuscript location, explain the consequence, and propose a feasible remedy. Apply methods checks relevant to this paper rather than a universal checklist. Request new data, controls, replications, or citations only when they could resolve a material uncertainty; consider whether narrowing a claim would suffice. Apply the same standard to null results.

Give a short summary, specific strengths, ranked concerns, and a candid overall assessment. Scale the detail to the request. When synthesizing separate reviews, keep their first assessments independent and check disagreements against the manuscript and sources before adopting them.

Adapted from [King and Worthington's reviewer guidance](https://gking.harvard.edu/files/peerless-supp.pdf) and [Brendan Nyhan's peer-review checklist](https://polmeth.org/blog/checklist-manifesto-peer-review).
