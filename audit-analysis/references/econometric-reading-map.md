# Econometric reading map

Use this map to route a concrete problem to a methodological tradition. Read primary sources when the audit turns on the exact method or assumption.

- Andrew Gelman: Type S and Type M errors, the significance filter, multilevel modeling, prior and posterior predictive checks, fake-data simulation, and the garden of forking paths.
- Donald Green: design-based experimental analysis, treatment fidelity, attrition, randomization inference, clustering at assignment, and pre-specified covariate adjustment.
- Leslie Kish, Peter Westfall, James MacKinnon, Matthew Webb, and A. Colin Cameron: few-cluster inference, wild-cluster bootstrap choices, leverage, and treated-cluster limits.
- Guido Imbens: potential outcomes, overlap, design before analysis, local estimands, external validity, and careful separation of identification from estimation.
- Susan Athey: honest estimation, cross-fitting, treatment-effect heterogeneity, policy learning, and the distinction between prediction and causal effects.
- David Freedman: regression adjustment assumptions, model-based standard errors in experiments, and skepticism toward automatic regression repair.
- Alberto Abadie: when clustering is justified, synthetic controls, and design-specific sources of uncertainty.
- Charles Manski: partial identification, bounds, missing outcomes, and decisions under ambiguity.
- Judea Pearl and Peter Spirtes: causal graphs, adjustment sets, mediators, colliders, and testable implications.
- Joshua Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke: transparent estimands, first stages, reduced forms, and design-based credibility.
- Jonathan Roth, Pedro Sant'Anna, Alyssa Bilinski, and John Poe: pretrend power, staggered difference-in-differences, and honest sensitivity to trend violations.
- Matias Cattaneo and coauthors: regression-discontinuity construction, bandwidth choice, bias correction, manipulation, and local-randomization alternatives.
- Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett: omitted-variable sensitivity in the scale of partial R-squared.
- Lee Cronbach, Richard McDonald, and contemporary psychometric work: internal consistency, omega, dimensionality, measurement invariance, and the separation of reliability from validity.

Names are routing devices, not appeals to authority. Convert every idea into a check with a number, graph, bound, simulation, or identified assumption.
